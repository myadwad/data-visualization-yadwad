# The final data story
[My Final Project: Filling the Gaps in Global Health Funding](https://carnegiemellon.shorthandstories.com/filling-the-gaps-in-global-health-funding/index.html)

# Changes made since Part II
I made several changes since Part II and rewrote my narrative multiple times. I redrafted my narrative and storyboards because I realized that I had too much data and too many data visualizations that were not relevant to my target audience - NGOs based in South Africa, Ethiopia, and Nigeria. (I took the advice of my critique interviewees to change my audience from policymakers AND NGO leaders to ONLY NGOs)  I had to think deeply about what visualizations would be relevant to my audience and which would have an impact. It was helpful to go back to my initial framing and class brainstorm exercises. I returned to the UN SDGs as a focal point of my narrative and as the 'Why' for this topic. SDG 3 talks about ending the epidemics of AIDS, TB, and Malaria and sub-Saharan Africa carries the highest burden of these diseases. I had to remind myself of this 'Why' during the design process. 

During this process, I reduced the number of visualizations from five to three. Upon completion of my final Shorthand, I realized that the map visualizing HIV, TB, and Malaria incidences in each country (emphasizing the HIV by color) may not be as impactful as I intended. A global map of other country and continent incidences to compare with my chosen three countries would have carried a stronger message in the urgency of this funding crisis. Nevertheless, I felt it was important to include a map and included it in my final draft. 

I was very interested in comparing external health funding to domestic funding in my chosen countries. This was a primary theme in my subject matter research and thus I wanted to capture this in a visualization. I created pie charts of national budgets to compare the proportion of health funding to the overall budget. I played with the animation feature on Tableau to showcase the differences between 2024/25 to 2025/26 budgets. This was a fun feature to play with, albeit a little challenging. This visualization required a lot of data cleaning in terms of finding the budgets and converting currencies. What was surprising but also a strong message is that country budgets did not shift significantly between years even with the loss of US funding! I think the lack of visible change through animation (for Nigeria and South Africa) emphasizes how much progress still needs to be made at the domestic level. Upon reflection, I think the pie chart would have been more effective had I included a few more budget sector slices potentially for other social welfare sectors, energy, and defense. 

I found myself very invested in developing a strong call to action with feasible recommendations for my target audience; this required a fair amount of research. The call to action section in my Shorthand is my weakest visual section. I believe it's not the most visually compelling but I felt it was important to focus on the actions; and therefore, I relied on button features and relevant background images to draw attention.
